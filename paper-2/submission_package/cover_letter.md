Please find enclosed our manuscript entitled "Do GNN Explanations Reflect Model Reasoning? A Counterfactual Evaluation of Causal Faithfulness in Molecular Toxicity Prediction" for consideration as a Research Paper in the Explainability/Interpretable Machine Learning section of Machine Learning with Applications.

We ask whether standard GNN training produces causally faithful explanations, as tested by a counterfactual-sufficiency protocol on molecular toxicity benchmarks. Our work addresses the critical gap between plausible and causally faithful explanations in graph neural networks, which is essential for trustworthy AI in safety-critical domains like drug discovery.

Our findings are significant for the machine learning community because they challenge the assumption that architectural choices alone ensure explanation faithfulness. We demonstrate through rigorous counterfactual evaluation that standard empirical risk minimization on a state-of-the-art GINE architecture does not induce alignment between attributed substructures and model decisions. This negative result is replicated across five seeds, three datasets, and multiple evaluation metrics, providing robust evidence that motivates the development of more principled objectives for causal alignment in GNNs.

The manuscript fits the scope of Machine Learning with Applications as it:
- Applies machine learning to a real-world problem (molecular toxicity prediction)
- Employs rigorous empirical methodology with statistical validation
- Addresses fundamental challenges in explainable AI
- Provides actionable insights for practitioners developing trustworthy ML systems
